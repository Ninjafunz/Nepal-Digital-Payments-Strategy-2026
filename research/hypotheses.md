# Hypotheses Document

**Project:** From Cash to Platforms: The Transformation of Nepal's Digital Payments Industry, 2021–2026

---

## Purpose

This document states 14 testable hypotheses — one per research theme. These are **propositions to investigate**, not findings. They will be tested against evidence and either supported, rejected, or refined.

**Important:** No data has been collected yet. These hypotheses are informed by general knowledge of digital payments markets in developing economies. They must be validated or invalidated by Nepal-specific evidence.

---

## Hypothesis Structure

Each hypothesis follows this format:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Hypothesis** | The testable proposition |
| **Null expectation** | What we would expect if the hypothesis is false |
| **Evidence needed** | Specific data required to test it |
| **Analytical method** | How we will test it |
| **Confidence** | Pre-data confidence level: High / Medium / Low |

---

## Theme 1: Digital Payment Adoption

### H1: Nepal is undergoing a structural shift from cash to digital payments, not merely an incremental increase in digital payment usage.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Digital payment growth is proportional to overall economic growth — no structural shift |
| **Evidence needed** | Digital payment share of total transactions (volume and value) growing faster than GDP; channel-specific adoption curves showing inflection points |
| **Analytical method** | Time-series analysis of digital/total payment ratio; comparison of digital payment CAGR vs. GDP growth; logistic adoption curve fitting |
| **Confidence** | High — consistent with regional trends, but magnitude is Nepal-specific |

---

## Theme 2: Transaction Volume and Value

### H2: Digital transaction value is growing faster than digital transaction count, indicating a shift toward higher-value use cases.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Transaction count and value grow at roughly the same rate |
| **Evidence needed** | Monthly/annual data on total digital transaction count and value; average transaction value over time |
| **Analytical method** | Comparison of count CAGR vs. value CAGR; average transaction value trend analysis; channel-level decomposition |
| **Confidence** | Medium — plausible if digital moves from P2P micro-transactions to merchant/government payments |

---

## Theme 3: Payment-Channel Evolution

### H3: QR-based payments are the fastest-growing payment channel in Nepal and are displacing card-based and cash-based transactions at the point of sale.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | QR growth is incremental and does not significantly displace other channels |
| **Evidence needed** | QR transaction volume and value growth rates vs. other channels; POS and card transaction trends; merchant acceptance data |
| **Analytical method** | Channel growth rate comparison; market share analysis over time; channel substitution analysis |
| **Confidence** | High — QR adoption has been rapid across South Asia, but Nepal-specific data is needed |

---

## Theme 4: Consumer Engagement

### H4: Wallet users are engaging more frequently than bank mobile-banking users, but with lower average transaction values.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Engagement patterns are similar across wallet and mobile-banking channels |
| **Evidence needed** | Transactions per user per month for wallets vs. mobile banking; average transaction value by channel |
| **Analytical method** | Engagement metric calculation (transactions/user); channel-level comparison; time-series trend analysis |
| **Confidence** | Medium — follows the global pattern for wallet vs. bank digital, but Nepal data needed |

---

## Theme 5: Merchant Ecosystem

### H5: Merchant-side adoption is the binding constraint on digital payment growth in Nepal — the ecosystem is demand-constrained, not supply-constrained.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Merchant adoption is keeping pace with consumer adoption |
| **Evidence needed** | Merchant acceptance rates by channel; consumer willingness-to-pay metrics; merchant onboarding trends; geographic merchant coverage |
| **Analytical method** | Supply-demand gap analysis; merchant-to-user ratios; geographic penetration comparison; qualitative assessment of merchant-side barriers |
| **Confidence** | Medium — common in developing markets, but Nepal may differ due to NRB mandates |

---

## Theme 6: Banks vs. Wallets vs. Payment Infrastructure

### H6: Wallet-based payment systems are capturing a disproportionate share of transaction growth relative to bank-led digital payments, despite banks holding the majority of deposits and accounts.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Banks and wallets are growing at similar rates relative to their existing base |
| **Evidence needed** | Wallet transaction volume/value vs. bank mobile/internet banking transaction volume/value; relative growth rates; user overlap data |
| **Analytical method** | Comparative growth analysis; market share trends; channel-level decomposition of total digital payments |
| **Confidence** | Medium — hypothesis is directional, not absolute; evidence may show convergence |

---

## Theme 7: Network Effects

### H7: Nepal's digital payment market exhibits cross-side network effects (more users attract more merchants and vice versa), but these effects have not yet reached a tipping point that would create winner-take-most dynamics.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Network effects are weak or non-existent; user growth is driven by factors other than network size |
| **Evidence needed** | Correlation between user growth and merchant growth; user concentration metrics; switching cost indicators; interoperability impact data |
| **Analytical method** | Cross-side correlation analysis; HHI calculation by channel; switching cost assessment; before/after analysis of interoperability mandates |
| **Confidence** | Low — network effects are hard to measure directly; this hypothesis may require proxy indicators |

---

## Theme 8: Customer Ownership

### H8: The "owner" of the customer relationship in digital payments is shifting from banks (who hold the account) to platforms (who hold the interface and data), as users interact primarily through wallet and super-app interfaces.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Banks retain primary customer ownership through account primacy |
| **Evidence needed** | User interface data (which app is opened first); transaction initiation patterns; data access and sharing arrangements; customer overlap data |
| **Analytical method** | Interface analysis; customer journey mapping; data access assessment; qualitative industry interviews (Phase 6) |
| **Confidence** | Low — this is the most conceptually complex hypothesis and may not be directly testable with quantitative data alone |

---

## Theme 9: Value-Chain Economics

### H9: The per-transaction economics of digital payments in Nepal are negative or marginal for most participants, and profitability depends on scale, float income, and adjacent services rather than transaction fees.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Transaction fees alone are sufficient for profitability at observed volumes |
| **Evidence needed** | Fee structures by channel; operating cost data; float income estimates; revenue diversification evidence from company disclosures |
| **Analytical method** | Unit economics estimation; cost-revenue gap analysis; comparison with regional benchmarks; revenue model decomposition |
| **Confidence** | Medium — consistent with global digital payment economics, but Nepal-specific cost structures needed |

---

## Theme 10: Potential Profit Pools

### H10: The largest profit pools in Nepal's payments ecosystem are shifting from traditional banking fees (interchange, account charges) to data monetization, merchant services, and value-added financial products.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Traditional fee-based revenue remains the dominant profit pool |
| **Evidence needed** | Revenue breakdowns by source for major players; merchant services revenue growth; lending/ad Insurance cross-sell data; data monetization evidence |
| **Analytical method** | Profit pool estimation (PvP framework); revenue source decomposition; comparison of revenue mix over time |
| **Confidence** | Low — requires company-level financial data that may be limited for private PSPs |

---

## Theme 11: Regulatory Influence

### H11: NRB regulatory actions (interoperability mandates, licensing, transaction limits) have been the single most important driver of competitive structure in Nepal's digital payments market.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Regulatory changes have had limited impact; market forces are the primary competitive drivers |
| **Evidence needed** | Timeline of NRB regulatory actions; competitive structure changes before/after key regulations; market response to specific mandates |
| **Analytical method** | Event study methodology; before/after analysis of key regulatory changes; regulatory impact mapping |
| **Confidence** | Medium — NRB is clearly active, but quantifying its relative importance vs. market forces is challenging |

---

## Theme 12: Strategic Groups

### H12: Nepal's digital payments industry contains 3–4 distinct strategic groups defined by (a) customer base type, (b) transaction volume vs. value positioning, and (c) degree of financial product diversification.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | The industry is homogeneous or cannot be meaningfully segmented into strategic groups |
| **Evidence needed** | Multi-dimensional data on major players: transaction volume, value, user base, product range, revenue sources; clustering analysis |
| **Analytical method** | Multi-dimensional scaling or cluster analysis; strategic group map construction; validation against qualitative industry knowledge |
| **Confidence** | Medium — strategic groups likely exist, but identifying the right dimensions requires data |

---

## Theme 13: Competitive Advantage

### H13: The primary source of competitive advantage in Nepal's digital payments market is shifting from regulatory access (licenses, permissions) to network scale (user and merchant base) and data capabilities.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Regulatory access remains the primary competitive advantage |
| **Evidence needed** | Timeline of competitive advantages by player; correlation between user/merchant scale and financial performance; data capability indicators |
| **Analytical method** | Advantage source classification over time; performance correlation analysis; dynamic capability assessment |
| **Confidence** | Low — competitive advantage is inherently qualitative and may resist purely quantitative measurement |

---

## Theme 14: Industry Scenarios for 2027–2030

### H14: The most likely industry configuration for 2030 is a "platform consolidation" scenario in which 2–3 platforms dominate digital payments, but regulatory intervention (interoperability mandates) will prevent full monopoly.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Null expectation** | Multiple plausible scenarios exist with roughly equal probability; no single scenario dominates |
| **Evidence needed** | Current market concentration trends; regulatory trajectory; technology development; consumer behavior trends; regional comparison |
| **Analytical method** | Scenario construction based on key uncertainties; leading indicator mapping; Monte Carlo or sensitivity analysis if data permits |
| **Confidence** | Low — scenario analysis is inherently forward-looking and speculative; value is in the framework, not the prediction |

## How These Hypotheses Will Be Used

1. **Phase 3–4 (Data Engineering):** Data collection and validation will be guided by what each hypothesis requires
2. **Phase 5 (Core Analysis):** Each analysis will explicitly test one or more hypotheses
3. **Phase 7 (Strategic Analysis):** Hypothesis results will feed into competitive positioning analysis
4. **Phase 8 (Scenarios):** Tested hypotheses will inform scenario construction
5. **Final Report:** Each hypothesis will be stated, tested, and concluded
